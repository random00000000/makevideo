## 
One sentence in. *A documentary out.*
> Here is the ambitious goal. One sentence in. A documentary out.

## The goal
**15 min**
to turn one sentence into a ten-minute, channel-quality documentary, on a laptop
> Specifically: one sentence becomes a ten minute, channel quality documentary, in under fifteen minutes, on an ordinary laptop. Here is how we get there, in three phases.

## Chapter: Phase one, faster edits
> Phase one. Faster edits.

## Phase one
1. Scene cache: re-render only what changed
2. Visual QA: the agent reviews its own frames
3. Narrators: a deeper voice option for history
> Three features. First, a scene cache. Change one line, and only that scene re-renders, in seconds. Second, visual QA. After rendering, the agent checks its own frames for overflowing text, unreadable contrast, and wrong images. Third, more narrators, like a deeper voice for history, while the current voice stays the default.

## Chapter: Phase two, the Kings and Generals look
> Phase two. The Kings and Generals look.

## Phase two
1. Animated maps: territories, borders, marching arrows
2. Drawn diagrams: the agent writes vector art, it animates
3. Real footage: public-domain clips by search
> Phase two is the signature look. First, animated maps, with territories that grow and arrows that march. Second, drawn diagrams, sketched by the agent as vector art and animated stroke by stroke. Third, real footage, found by search in public domain archives.

## Chapter: Phase three, one sentence
> Phase three. One sentence.

## Phase three
1. Research mode: sources, facts and citations first
2. Shorts from long videos, cut automatically
3. Other languages from the same script
> Phase three makes the one sentence real. First, a research mode, which gathers sources and checked facts before writing a word, with citations in the description. Second, shorts cut automatically from every long video. Third, the same script narrated in other languages.
