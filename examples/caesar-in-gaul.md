## Caesar in Gaul
![Julius Caesar Tusculum portrait]()
58 to 50 BC
> In 58 BC, a politician drowning in debt took command of four legions on the edge of the Roman world. Eight years later, he had conquered Gaul, and was ready to conquer Rome itself. His name was Gaius Julius Caesar.

## Chapter: The gamble
![Roman legionaries reenactment]()
> Chapter one. The gamble.

## The deal
- Five years of command
- Four veteran legions
- An excuse he still needed
> Caesar had just finished his year as consul, and he had bought his way there. Through his allies Pompey and Crassus, he secured an unusual prize. Five years of command over the Gallic provinces. Four veteran legions loyal to him. All he lacked was an excuse for war.

## The excuse
**368,000** people on the move, by Caesar's own count
> The excuse arrived that same spring. A Celtic people, the Helvetii, burned their own villages and set out to migrate west. Caesar claimed they numbered three hundred and sixty eight thousand.

## Two enemies, one summer
![Battle of Bibracte]()
The Helvetii and Ariovistus, 58 BC
> Caesar blocked their path at the Rhone, then crushed them near Bibracte. Weeks later, he turned on Ariovistus, a Germanic king the Senate had once called a friend of Rome, and drove him back across the Rhine. Two campaigns, one summer.

## Chapter: Beyond the edges
![Caesar's Rhine bridge]()
> Chapter two. Beyond the edges of the map.

## Timeline
- 57 BC: The Belgae and the Nervii
- 56 BC: A sea battle off Brittany
- 55 BC: A bridge across the Rhine
- 55 to 54 BC: Two landings in Britain
> Each year, Caesar pushed further. In 57 BC, he beat the Belgae, fighting in the front line himself against the Nervii. In 56 BC, his ships beat the Veneti at sea. In 55 BC, his engineers bridged the Rhine in about ten days. And twice, he landed in Britain.

## Legions under Caesar (approx.)
<!-- highlight: 52 BC -->
- 58 BC, spring: 4
- 58 BC, summer: 6
- 57 BC: 8
- 52 BC: 10
> And his army kept growing. He started with four legions. By that summer he had six. A year later, eight. And by 52 BC, about ten, far more than the Senate had ever given him.

## Chapter: The revolt
![Vercingetorix statue]()
> Chapter three. The revolt.

## Vercingetorix
![Vercingetorix Clermont-Ferrand]()
A young Arvernian noble
> In 52 BC, Gaul rose as one. Its leader was Vercingetorix, a young noble of the Arverni. He burned his own towns and fields so the legions would starve, and at Gergovia, he handed Caesar one of the worst defeats of his career.

## Alesia
Caesar built *two walls*.
> Then came Alesia. Vercingetorix took refuge in a hilltop town, and Caesar did something astonishing. He built two walls.

## The siege
- An inner wall to trap the town
- An outer wall against the relief army
- Caesar claimed 250,000 came to the rescue
> One wall faced inward, to trap the defenders. A second faced outward, to hold off the army coming to rescue them. Caesar claimed a quarter of a million Gauls arrived. They broke against his walls.

## The surrender
![Vercingetorix throws down his arms]()
> Starving and out of hope, Vercingetorix rode out and laid his weapons at Caesar's feet. He would spend six years in a Roman prison, before being paraded through Rome in Caesar's triumph, and executed.

## The cost
**1,000,000** dead, according to Plutarch
> The cost was staggering. Plutarch wrote that a million Gauls died, and a million more were enslaved. Modern historians think those numbers are exaggerated. But the devastation was real.

## The real prize
Gaul was the stepping stone. *Rome was the prize.*
> For Caesar, Gaul was only the stepping stone. In 49 BC, he led his legions across the Rubicon river into Italy. Rome was the prize.
